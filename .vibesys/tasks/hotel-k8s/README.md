# hotel-k8s task files

Task-owned inputs for the Hotel Reservation Kubernetes task. `OBJECTIVE.md` is for
the optimizing agents; this file is for whoever maintains the task. Nothing here
is a candidate input: the CLI versions these files as the task.

## What the benchmark does (bench.py, one sample)

bench.py first validates the candidate's application tier (`envelope.load`), makes sure the
node holds the data snapshot (`snapshot.ensure`: the data Job builds it the first time, about
25 minutes, and otherwise checks it in seconds) and writes the composed manifest with the
snapshot's version; a violation or a snapshot failure fails the sample before anything is
deployed, with its message. vibesys-evaluator then deploys `SYSTEM` (builds `hotelReservation/`
and the task image in-cluster, fresh namespace, the `tier=data` Deployments, whose MongoDB pods
copy the snapshot into their emptyDirs, and every Service and ConfigMap, the seed Job running
`seed`, which checks what the servers hold, the candidate's Deployments, readiness probes
through `frontend:5000`),
then runs
`benchmark(run)`: two closed-loop phases (32 clients, 15 s warmup, 60 s; throughput = their
median) and three open-loop phases at 0.7 x that throughput (uniform arrivals, 10 s warmup,
60 s; p99 = their median). Each phase is a pod of the task image: loadgen runs
`hotel-generator generate`, which writes the phase's request stream from the dataset and the
sample's seed for as long as loadgen reads it. A benchmark only measures: nothing checks the
answers (the accuracy check does), and an answer with another status than the operation expects
is an error. A sample fails on any loadgen error in a window, an operation of the mix that never
completed, or an open loop below 95% of its rate. The sample seed is drawn per sample and
recorded (`seeds.json`; `bench.py benchmark --seed N` replays one). The application's writes
carry from phase to phase, so nothing is reset; each phase builds its model afresh and draws its
reservation nights and added hotels from its own block (`BeginPhase`), so its requests stay
valid whatever the earlier phases wrote.

## What the accuracy check does (bench.py accuracy)

`bench.py accuracy` runs `prepare` (the capacity envelope, then the data snapshot):
a candidate outside the envelope fails (exit 1), a snapshot that cannot be made ready is UNKNOWN
(exit 2). vibesys-evaluator's accuracy run then deploys the system as a benchmark sample does
(builds, fresh namespace, data tier restored from the snapshot, the seed's check, the
candidate's tier, readiness probes), runs four open-loop phases of `hotel-accuracy-generator`
(200 operations/s, 2 s warmup, 30 s; about 26,000 operations) with every answer captured,
applies the faults between them, tears the namespace down and runs `hotel-oracle --history DIR`
on the captured history. Exit 0 PASS, 1 FAIL, 2 UNKNOWN (also when the run cannot complete);
the report, the failing partitions' timelines and the history are in `accuracy/` of the job's
artifacts.

Faults (the oracle's `Fault` is nil: none of them may change what a read returns):

- after phase 0, one pod of each of the candidate's Deployments, one after the other, each
  waited for until the application serves again (the other replicas serve meanwhile);
- after phase 1, the three memcached tiers emptied in place (`Fault.flush`: memcached's
  `flush_all` in every pod);
- after phase 2, every pod of each of the candidate's Deployments at once, one Deployment after
  the other.

The candidate's Deployments are the ones its `deploy/app.yaml` declares (a regrouped tier is
restarted as it is grouped). MongoDB is never restarted: its pods restore the snapshot into an
emptyDir, so a new pod would lose the run's writes by the task's design, not the candidate's.

The accuracy workload (`evaluator/internal/hotelaccuracy`) meets the oracle's contract without
exclusive keys: a reservation is one night; reservations contend on six hot hotels, on a
two-night window that slides through the phase so nights fill up and later reservations are
refused (rooms in chunks of a third to a sixth of the capacity, or one room); four
capacity-managed hotels form two pairs that swap roles every phase, one pair taking capacity
writes (15% of them below the fullest night: 409) while the other takes reservations, so a
capacity write never overlaps a reservation of its hotel; 20% of the stays from phase 1 on are
nights an earlier phase booked, read and reserved again across the fault. Searches, rate and
recommendation reads, profile and price writes go to the hot hotels; logins (60% with the right
password). The rate is fixed and modest so that the answers are judged, not the capacity: more
than 1% of unknown outcomes (lost answers and 5xx) fails.

## Accuracy oracle

`evaluator/cmd/oracle` (`hotel-oracle`) is the task's semantic oracle on the vibesys-evaluator
checker (`check`, `sdk/vibesys-evaluator/docs/accuracy-checks.md`): `check.Main` over one
`check.Spec`, built by `internal/hotelspec` (`hotelspec.Spec(catalog)`).

- **Partition key**: the hotel (`h:<id>`). A search (up to 5 hotels) and a recommendation are
  projected into one piece per hotel they could return, each checked in its hotel's history (no
  cross-hotel snapshot is promised). Logins, GeoJSON fields and the hotel sets of
  recommendations depend on no state and are checked while projecting (a `Violation`); their
  pieces are keyless (counted in the coverage and the unknown-outcome share, never searched).
- **Model** (`Project`, `Init`, `Step`, `EffectWindow` 10 s): reservations and capacity writes
  are strict; a reservation takes the rooms of its night or none, a failed login still reserves
  (the pinned frontend), a capacity below the fullest night is 409. Everything search,
  recommendations and `/rates` show is under the 1 s bound, strictly more than 1 s: profile
  fields and prices are `check.Window`, each night's rooms a `check.Counter`, capacity a
  `check.Register`, nights a `check.PMap`. A lost response or 5xx is an unknown outcome.
- **Faults**: `Spec.Fault` is nil. A restart of the application tier or a memcached flush
  changes no state, since an acknowledged write is durable (MongoDB holds it, the caches are
  rebuilt from it): a reservation, profile or rate lost across a fault shows up as a later
  answer the model cannot reach.
- **Contract with the accuracy workload** (`internal/hotelspec/info.go` lists each operation's
  Info and HTTP step): reservations stay at one night (the base gives a multi-night stay's rooms
  back night by night, so a stay is not atomic to observers); a capacity write never overlaps a
  reservation of the same hotel (the base orders them only when they do not overlap); at most
  about 12 operations in flight on one hotel (`MaxInFlight` in the report); lost or 5xx answers
  under 1% (`--max-lost-share`).
- **Not covered**: `POST /admin/hotel` (the oracle has no model of additions; the benchmark
  still sends them) and multi-night stays.
- **Tests**: `go test ./internal/hotelspec` (synthetic histories from a simulated application:
  correct runs pass; an overbooking race, reservations lost after a restart, availability stale
  beyond 1 s, a search that shows a full hotel, profile writes and reservations lost by a
  flush fail; crafted edges: the bound, a failed login, unknown outcomes, faults, violations,
  keyless pieces). `go test ./internal/hotelaccuracy` checks the workload's side of the
  contract. `HOTEL_ORACLE_CAPTURES=<harness/runs of the oracle spike> go test -v ./cmd/oracle`
  checks the spike's real captures offline (base1 and base2 must pass, mutant1 must fail; about
  2.5 GB, 10-40 s each).

## Dataset

The data is a pure function of `dataset.py`'s `SPEC` (format 3, seed `"hotel-v3"`,
1,500,000 hotels, 500,000 users; changing any field changes the task), generated by
`evaluator/internal/dataset` in about 3 s and 150 MB: each hotel's compact core (location,
rating, price, capacity, city, plan count) is kept, its full record and its 730 nights of
bookings are regenerated from the hotel's own random stream when asked for (7 us and 27 us).
The generator, the oracle, the snapshot builder and the seed's check each generate it from
the spec.

| Collection | Documents | In MongoDB (data + indexes, uncompressed) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `geo-db.geo` | 1,500,000 | ~0.15 GB |
| `profile-db.hotels` | 1,500,000 | ~0.7 GB |
| `rate-db.inventory` | 3,971,724 | ~1.0 GB |
| `recommendation-db.recommendation` | 1,500,000 | ~0.2 GB |
| `reservation-db.number` | 1,500,000 | ~0.1 GB |
| `reservation-db.nights` | 1,090,572,136 | ~109 GB (73 B a document, ~27 B of each index) |
| `reservation-db.reservation` | 0 (index only) | 0 |
| `user-db.user` | 500,000 | ~0.06 GB |

About 112 GB (104 GiB) in all, about 52 GB on disk (snappy). Against it: the application's
8 GiB, 6 x 0.25 GiB of WiredTiger cache and 3 x 64 MiB of memcached, 9.7 GiB, so the data is
about 11x the memory a candidate can cache documents in (8x counting memcached's 1 GiB
containers instead of its 64 MiB of items; 4.5x counting every MongoDB pod's full 2 GiB, page
cache included). What can fit is a compact projection: a bitmap or small counters of the
nights, names and phones without the documents around them. That is a design a production
system could choose and is allowed; holding the documents is not possible.

- 20 real metropolitan areas; hotels distributed over them by Zipf rank (London 416,929 ...
  Rio de Janeiro 20,846), around each centre with normal offsets whose spread grows with the
  square root of the hotel count (London about 31 km, Rio about 16 km); unique coordinates
  with 6 decimals; IDs `1`..`1500000` in a random order of cities.
- Profiles: name, phone, address and description from per-city templates; float32 coordinates
  (the profile service refuses to truncate float64).
- 1-5 rate plans per hotel (mean 2.65): `RACK` over the whole 2027-2028 rate window, other
  plans over parts of it; prices by city, star class and plan; tax-inclusive rates.
- Recommendation attributes: rating 1 + 4 Beta(5, 2) at 0.01, price = the cheapest plan;
  capacities 10-800 rooms by star class; a popularity rank per hotel within its city.
- Seeded bookings (`reservation-db.nights`, `{hotelId, night, rooms}`, a night with none has
  no document): per hotel a base occupancy of 30-85%, a seasonal swing (+-15%, peak in July),
  weekday factors, fewer bookings further ahead (down 55% over the window) and noise, rounded
  and clamped to [0, capacity - 1]: 42% of the rooms booked on average, never a full night, so
  a 1-room search over the seeded calendar finds every hotel available as before. The oracle's
  409 model of `POST /admin/capacity` counts them, and the workload's capacity writes stay
  above each hotel's seeded maximum.
- 500,000 users with unique names and random 10-16 character passwords (stored as SHA-256).
- Indexes on the services' query keys (`id`, `hotelId`, hotel+night unique, `username`), and
  geo's and recommendation's `added_1`, built with the data so no service builds one at start.

Requests (`evaluator/internal/hotelworkload`) draw from the operation's own stream: locations by city popularity
(Zipf, s = 1) with the hotels' spread around the centre (5 decimals; a search redraws where
geo's own ranking ties), stays of 1-7 nights skewed short over the rate window, logins and
reservation users by Zipf (s = 0.75) over the users, rates, reservations and writes by hotel
popularity; reservations on far-future nights of the phase's own block (1/16 of a block the
sample draws), at most 128 distinct hotels a night, each on a (night, hotel) slot of its own, so
no two reservations of a phase contend and none is serialized.

## Writes in the workload

About 1% of the mix (104 of 10,504 weight) are owner writes, plus `hotel_rates` reads of
`GET /rates` (4%):

| Operation | Weight | Target and value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `update_rate` | 42 | a hotel by popularity, one of its plans; prices x0.8-1.25 of the seeded ones |
| `update_profile` | 42 | a hotel by popularity; a new phone, and in 10% of the updates a new name |
| `set_capacity` | 12 | a capacity-managed hotel (one in four, never booked by `reserve_capacity`) not yet written in this phase; rooms between C/2 and 2C, then a reservation of rooms+1 (refused) |
| `add_hotel` | 8 | a hotel in the sample's sandbox cell (below) |

Nothing serializes the writes (the benchmark checks no answer, so it needs no order of
versions): rate and profile writes of one hotel may overlap. A hotel takes at most one capacity
write a phase, so no two of them overlap: the probe after a write could otherwise meet a
concurrent, larger capacity, take its rooms and make the hotel's later capacity writes 409, an
error of the window. Sandboxes keep additions from changing seeded answers: about
31,000 cells of 25 km in the South Pacific, more than 1,000 km from every seeded hotel; a sample
adds its hotels within 2 km of one cell's centre (IDs `a<hex>-<n>`, numbered from the phase's
own block), and 3% of the searches and distance recommendations query near an addition of the
same phase.

## Base-application changes

These changes to `hotelReservation/` are part of the base every candidate starts from:

- `deploy/app.yaml`: the application tier (moved from `k8s/manifest.yaml`, the reference
  topology unchanged); the Dockerfile copies named directories only, so the file does not
  change the image's build layers.
- `cmd/*/db.go`: a service inserts its built-in test data only into an empty collection
  (reservation-db is keyed on its capacity collection).
- `services/rate`: on a cache miss, GetRates reads the missed hotel's plans instead of the
  whole inventory, which made every search return every hotel that has a plan.
- `services/reservation`: CheckAvailability read cached capacities under the wrong key and
  never ran its MongoDB fallbacks, so search hid hotels with rooms free (fix with a test).
- The write path: `services/frontend/admin.go` (routes above), new RPCs `UpdateRate`/`AddRates`,
  `UpdateProfile`/`AddProfile`, `SetCapacity`/`AddCapacity`, `geo.AddHotel`,
  `recommendation.AddHotel` (protos regenerated with protoc 25.0 and the
  protoc-gen-go/-grpc versions named in the generated headers). Each owner writes MongoDB, then deletes the memcached key.
- `lease/`: cache fills take a memcached lease (`add` a placeholder, then `cas` the value),
  so a fill that read MongoDB before a write cannot overwrite the write's invalidation.
- `changes/`: geo and recommendation keep their in-memory catalog, now under a RWMutex; an
  addition inserts the document (`added: true`) and bumps a version counter in
  `<db>.changes`, and every replica polls the counter every 200 ms and reloads the added
  documents when it moved. Without writes this costs five point reads a second per replica.
- `internal/memcfake` (an in-process memcached for tests), `internal/writetest` (end-to-end
  write tests of the services against a MongoDB given by `HOTEL_TEST_MONGO`).
- Not-found profiles are skipped instead of returned as null and cached.
- `services/reservation`: reserved rooms are counted per hotel and night in
  `reservation-db.nights` (unique index on hotelId + night, created at startup), and a night
  is taken by a conditional increment (`rooms <= capacity - n`), so MongoDB serializes
  concurrent reservations across instances; a stay that does not fit gives back the nights it
  took, and the cached counts are invalidated. Before, every request read the count
  (memcached, or a sum of reservation documents), compared and wrote: two replicas
  acknowledged 40 one-room reservations of a 10-room night
  (`internal/writetest.TestConcurrentReservationsAcrossReplicas`). A capacity change is still
  not ordered with reservations of the same hotel that run concurrently with it (the workload
  never does that: capacity writes go to hotels it never books).

## State per instance

With two instances of every service, per-process state was reviewed: `geo` and
`recommendation` keep their catalog in memory and poll `<db>.changes` every 200 ms for
additions (step 2); `user` loads the users once at startup, which stays correct because no
route changes users; `search` and `frontend` keep no state between requests; `profile`,
`rate` and `reservation` cache in the shared memcached (lease-guarded fills), with MongoDB as
the source of truth (reservation counts now included, above).

## Data snapshot

A sample does not insert 1.1 billion documents: that alone takes about 25 minutes. The data
is built once per dataset version into MongoDB data files on the node and copied by each
sample.

- Version: `snapshot.Version` (`hotel-data version`), 20 hex digits of SHA-256 over the
  snapshot layout, the mongo image, the spec, every collection's name, indexes and count, and
  the BSON of every document of 63 spread hotels and users. A generator, collection or image
  change names a new snapshot; nothing else does.
- Where: on the node's local disk (kind keeps the node's `/var` there), `/var/lib/hotel-k8s/
  snapshots/<version>/<database>/{db/, COMPLETE}`, plus `.lock` and `last-used`.
- Build: `snapshot.ensure` (bench.py's `prepare` for the benchmark and the accuracy check, and `python snapshot.py ensure`) builds the
  data image (the Dockerfile's `data` stage, tagged by the digest of `Dockerfile` and
  `evaluator/`) and runs Job `data-*` in namespace `hk8s-data` (outside every sample's, so a
  build outlives a killed job; requests 2 CPU / 4 GiB, limits 24 CPU / 24 GiB, 3 h deadline):
  `hotel-data ensure` takes the version's flock (concurrent Jobs serialize), runs one mongod
  per missing database on a fresh directory (`--nojournal`), creates the collections and
  indexes, inserts in index key order from several workers, writes a marker document naming
  the version, shuts mongod down cleanly (SIGTERM, a full checkpoint), writes `COMPLETE` and
  renames the directory into place. Versions unused for 72 h are deleted. A sample waits up to
  45 minutes for a build, then fails while the Job carries on for the next sample.
- Restore: each MongoDB pod's init container (the same mongo image) checks `COMPLETE` and
  copies `db/` into the pod's emptyDir, then mongod starts on it with journaling. Every
  sample starts from the same files and its writes die with its namespace.
- Check: the seed Job runs `hotel-data verify`: every server's marker names the version,
  every collection has its indexes and count, and the documents of 200 random hotels and
  users equal the generator's.

Measured locally (mongod 5.0 in a container on 16 CPUs of this host's NVMe disk): 15,000
hotels (1%) built in 19 s, 10.9M nights at 640k documents/s, 0.5 GB on disk; 150,000 hotels
(10%) in 135 s, 109M nights at a steady 830k documents/s, 5.2 GB on disk; its restore (copy
of all six databases, six mongods started, verify) took 15 s, the verify 2 s. Extrapolated to
the task's 1.5M hotels: about 22-28 minutes and 52 GB for the build, one to two minutes for a
sample's copy. At full scale (49 GB on disk, built on the test clusters) the six copies at
once outlasted vibesys-evaluator's default 300 s deploy wait in 3 of 9 deploys on
vibesys-k8s-2, so bench.py sets `k8s.App(wait_secs=1800)`.

## Capacity envelope

The candidate owns the application tier, `hotelReservation/deploy/app.yaml` (OBJECTIVE.md,
Resources). `envelope.py` checks it before any build or deploy, collecting every violation
into one error that names the object and key (`Deployment geo: spec.replicas: 1: at least 2`):

| Rule | Where |
| --- | --- |
| CPU limits over every container of every replica at most 16, memory at most 8 GiB | `validate`, the `envelope` problems |
| `requests` == `limits`, both of `cpu` and `memory` only, at least 100m and 64 MiB, whole millicores | `_Checker.resources` |
| `replicas` an integer >= 2 | `_Checker.deployment` |
| `GOMAXPROCS` set once: ceil(CPU limit) as a literal, or `resourceFieldRef` `limits.cpu` divisor 1 of the container itself | `_Checker.env` |
| `image: ${HOTEL_IMAGE}` only; no variables but `${NAMESPACE}` and `${HOTEL_IMAGE}` | `_Checker.container`, `validate` |
| kinds `Deployment` (`apps/v1`), `Service`, `ConfigMap` (`v1`); namespace `${NAMESPACE}` or absent; no `tier`/`backend` labels | `validate`, `_Checker.metadata` |
| allowlists of Deployment, pod, container, securityContext, port, env, volume (`configMap`, `emptyDir`) and Service (`ClusterIP`, headless) fields; `affinity`, `tolerations`, `nodeSelector` empty; default service account | `*_KEYS`, `_Checker.pod`, `_Checker.service` |
| no Deployment, Service or ConfigMap name of the task's; mounted ConfigMaps exist | `_Checker.deployment`, `_Checker.service`, `validate` |
| selectors select their own pods only; no pod matches a task Service's selector except `frontend`'s; at least one Deployment behind `frontend` | `_Checker.deployment`, `validate` |

Decisions: GOMAXPROCS is the CPU limit rounded up, because that is what the downward API
(`limits.cpu`, divisor 1) gives and what the base uses; a candidate may also write the literal.
Candidate ConfigMaps are allowed (a merged service may want its own configuration); Secrets,
PVCs, PodDisruptionBudgets and StatefulSets are not. The `frontend` Service stays the task's,
so its name, port and selector cannot change; any Deployment whose pods carry its labels serves
it. A candidate without `app.yaml` (from before the envelope) gets `k8s/app.yaml`, the base
topology, so its numbers are those it had.

## Resource limits

Requests equal limits on every container (Guaranteed QoS). Application tier: at most 16 CPUs
and 8 GiB in total, split by the candidate (the base: 16 pods, 8 services x 2, at 1 CPU, with
the memory as in `k8s/app.yaml`). MongoDB: 2 CPU, 2 GiB each, `--wiredTigerCacheSizeGB 0.25`
(the rest of the pod's memory is mongod's own and the page cache). memcached: 1 CPU, 1 GiB
each, `-m 64` (the 64 MiB it always ran with: the image does not read the chart's
`MEMCACHED_CACHE_SIZE`). consul: 1 CPU, 512 MiB. jaeger: 2 CPU, 1 GiB. The namespace's
deployments request at most 34 CPUs and 24.5 GiB. Pods of a benchmark sample or an accuracy
check (vibesys-evaluator's): seed 4 CPU (before the application starts), probe 0.5 CPU, phase
(loadgen) 6 CPU, 4 GiB; the accuracy check's oracle runs in the job's own container after the
namespace is gone. Peak demand is 40 CPUs, on a 64-CPU node shared with other tasks' jobs, so
two of these jobs cannot run at once. A first build of the data snapshot adds the data Job (Burstable: 2 CPU / 4 GiB requested, up
to 24 CPU / 24 GiB used) for about 25 minutes; on disk the node needs the snapshot (52 GB) and
one copy per running sample.
